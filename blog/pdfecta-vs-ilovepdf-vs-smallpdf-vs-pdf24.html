The Core Difference: Where Does Your File Actually Go?

This is the single most important distinction between these tools, and it's worth understanding rather than just taking a claim at face value.

You can verify this yourself for any PDF tool with a simple test: fully load the tool's page, then disconnect your internet connection, and try using it. If it still works, the tool is genuinely processing your file locally. If it fails or shows an error, your file was being sent somewhere over the internet.

PDFECTA is built specifically to pass this test on every single tool. iLovePDF and PDF24's web versions will fail this test, since they upload to a server (though both offer separate desktop applications that do process locally). Smallpdf's web version also uploads files and will fail this test.

Why This Matters for What You're Actually Converting

Not every PDF task involves sensitive information. Compressing a museum brochure or converting a public flyer to JPG carries little real risk regardless of which tool you use. But a significant share of everyday PDF tasks involve documents people would rather not upload anywhere:

  • Signed contracts and legal agreements
  • Bank statements and tax documents
  • Medical records and insurance forms
  • Passport scans, IDs, and other identity documents
  • Client files under NDA or confidentiality agreements

For these categories specifically, the processing model matters more than any other feature comparison.

Where PDFECTA Doesn't Win — An Honest Look

A fair comparison acknowledges tradeoffs, not just advantages. Here's where the established competitors currently have an edge:

  • OCR (text recognition on scanned documents) — iLovePDF and Smallpdf have more mature OCR capabilities for extracting text from scanned images, since this is computationally heavy and harder to do well entirely client-side.
  • Advanced editing — directly editing existing text within a PDF (not just adding new content) is more developed in tools like Adobe Acrobat and to some extent iLovePDF's paid tier.
  • Brand recognition and mobile apps — Smallpdf and iLovePDF have dedicated, polished mobile apps and much larger user bases with years of refinement.
  • Cloud storage integrations — direct integration with Google Drive, Dropbox, and similar services is more built-out in the established competitors.

If your priority is maximum feature depth for occasional, non-sensitive tasks, the established players are genuinely strong options. If your priority is guaranteed privacy for everyday tasks — compress, merge, split, sign, convert, watermark — without any file ever leaving your device, that's specifically what PDFECTA is built for.

Pricing Comparison

If cost is your primary concern and you don't need OCR or advanced editing, PDF24 and PDFECTA are the two options that will never ask you to pay.
