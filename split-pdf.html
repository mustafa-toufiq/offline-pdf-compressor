Split Methods Explained

  • Split Every Page Individually — creates a separate single-page PDF for each page in your document. Useful when you need every page as its own file.
  • Split by Custom Page Ranges — divides your document into sections based on ranges you define. For example, a 30-page document split into 1-10, 11-20, 21-30 creates three 10-page files.

When Should You Split a PDF?

  • Separating chapters or sections from a large report
  • Extracting invoices or receipts from a combined scanned document
  • Dividing a multi-page contract into individual sections for review
  • Breaking large PDFs into smaller files for upload portals with size limits
  • Creating individual files from each page of a scanned book
  • Separating a presentation exported as PDF into individual slide files

Split PDF vs Extract Pages — What's the Difference?

Splitting a PDF divides the entire document into multiple separate files based on page ranges — the result is several new files. Extracting pages creates a single new PDF containing only the specific pages you select, leaving the original document completely untouched. If you need pages 3, 7, and 12 combined into one new file, use Extract Pages instead. If you want to divide the whole document into multiple sections, use Split PDF.

Does Splitting a PDF Affect Quality?

No. Splitting a PDF is a completely non-destructive operation. Pages are copied directly from the original document into the new files without any compression, re-encoding, or quality loss. Text, images, fonts, and formatting remain exactly as they were in the original.
